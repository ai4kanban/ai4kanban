# AI4Kanban positioning

The positioning every entry point quotes: README, homepage, share image, `llms.txt`, docs,
blog, email and deck. Settled in #770 and refined in #771; no separate copy confirmation is required.

## Core positioning

AI4Kanban is the AI project manager that manages your agent team. It organizes the work,
follows through on execution, checks the results, and brings you only what you need to know.
You set the direction, make the key decisions, and approve the results.

- **The bottleneck**: one person cannot personally brief, track and read the output of ten,
  twenty or a hundred agents. Attention runs out before the agents do.
- **The layer**: a management agent, or a team of them, sits between the person and the agents
  doing the work. The copy does not tie this to a fixed architecture.
- **The payoff**: the person manages larger agent teams and tackles larger projects, keeping
  their attention on direction and key decisions instead of supervising every agent.
- **The board**: the board is where the work lives, not the headline.

### Where each piece goes

- **Headline + subheadline**: homepage hero and share image, always together.
- **Short description**: repository description, README intro, and other standalone blurbs. A
  shorter cut must still say that AI manages the agent team.
- **Narrative**: README body, docs overview, `llms.txt`, blog, email and deck.
- **Philosophy**: README philosophy section; 10× describes project scale, productivity or
  agent team capacity, never business growth or success.
- **Capability description**: wherever a reader decides whether it fits their work.
- **Search positioning**: use “AI project management for agent teams” in the homepage title.
  User-validated queries include “ai based project management”, “ai task management”,
  “kanban agent”, “ai team lead”, “ai team leader” and “intelligent project management”.
  Use them where relevant without reducing the product to task tracking or stuffing metadata.

## Audience and scenarios

- **Audience**: solo founders, creators and small teams handing real work to agents. Name them
  in the scope, never as the opening line.
- **Range**: product development to content marketing. It is a range, not a closed list.
- **Current examples**: development and demo video. Public examples must come from real
  deliveries.
- **Planned**: our own marketplace will distribute more workflows and scenarios. It is a plan;
  never present it as available.

## Claims

What the copy may say, and what backs it today.

| Claim | Backed by |
| --- | --- |
| Organizes the work | Planning breaks a card into steps and calls in specialist agents |
| Follows through | Deliveries run in the background and can be resumed when they stop |
| Checks the results | Agent review on development deliveries; demo video has no agent review |
| Reports what you need to know | Open questions, manual checks and deliveries waiting for approval |
| You approve the results | Verification checks left for the user, and deliveries that wait for approval before landing |

- **Scale is a vision**: 10×, 20× or 100× refers to project size, productivity or the agent
  team one person can manage, never business growth or success. Do not present these as
  measured gains, guarantees or concurrency limits, or equate agents with headcount.
- **Filtering is not hiding**: blockers and failures are always reported.
- **Never promise**: zero coding, suitability for everyone, or work with no human involved.
- **Writing is not sending**: producing copy or a video does not imply sending or publishing
  it. Name any external tool a scenario needs.

## Voice

- **Lead with management**: who runs the agents, and what that frees up for the person.
- **Short, strong sentences**: concrete verbs over feature lists, labels or architecture.
- **Human authority stays visible**: direction, key decisions and acceptance are the person's.
