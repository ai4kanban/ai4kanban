## Visiting the site

`ai4kanban.dev` is a static website. Every page is a file built ahead of time and served by Cloudflare Pages; there is no application server behind it and no database.

- **No analytics.** We run no analytics product on this site — not ours, not a third party's. We do not measure page views, sessions, scroll depth, or anything else about your visit.
- **No cookies.** The site sets no cookies of any kind, essential or otherwise, and stores nothing in your browser. There is no cookie banner because there is nothing to consent to.
- **No advertising or tracking pixels.** There are no ad networks, no data brokers, no retargeting tags, and no third-party trackers embedded in any page.
- **No accounts.** There is nothing to sign up for on this site, and no form that asks for your email address.

The one thing we do not control is the delivery itself. Cloudflare, as our hosting and content delivery provider, processes the technical request data that any web server sees — your IP address, the page requested, your user agent, and a timestamp — to route the request and protect the site from abuse. That processing is Cloudflare's own, under their published privacy documentation, and we do not receive, keep, or query those logs.

Pages on the site link out to other places, including GitHub. Once you follow a link, the site you land on is governed by its own privacy policy, not this one.

## Using the app

The AI4Kanban desktop app and the `akb` command run on your own machine. A board is a folder of Markdown files inside your own repository, and the app reads and writes those files locally. Nothing about your board, your project, your prompts, or your work is sent to us.

- **No account and no sign-in** are needed to install or use the app on a local board.
- **No usage data, telemetry, or crash reports** are collected by the app today.
- **The one network request the app makes on its own** is to `api.github.com`, once, to ask which release of AI4Kanban is newest, so it can tell you when a newer build is out. That request is unauthenticated, carries no identifier of yours, and sends nothing about your board or your machine. If it fails, the app carries on without a notice. GitHub sees the request as it would see any anonymous visitor: your IP address, a static user agent, and a timestamp.

The coding agent you point at your board is separate software with its own account, its own model provider, and its own privacy policy. It runs on your machine under your control, and what it sends to its provider is governed by that provider's terms — not by this policy.

## Using AI4Kanban Cloud

AI4Kanban Cloud is an **invite-only preview**. It exists so a team can share one authoritative board while every member keeps their code and their agent work on their own machine. Using it is a deliberate choice: you install the app on a local board, and Cloud is an option you pick afterwards. If you never choose it, nothing in this section applies to you.

### What a workspace stores

A Cloud workspace holds the board and only the board:

- **Cards** — every task's title, body, metadata, questions, and the answers to them, open and archived alike.
- **Project memory** — the memory files the board keeps: shipped work, settled decisions, rejected ideas, design notes, and the project goal.
- **Releases** — open and closed releases and their summaries.
- **The board's own configuration** — its name, tracks, modules, and per-flow rules.
- **History** — the board's counts and records, the delivery record of what each run built, and an audit trail attributing every change to the member who made it.
- **Membership** — who is in the workspace and what role each member holds.
- **Events and actions** — the questions, review requests, and approvals a member is asked to act on, and their outcomes.
- **A board's machine record** — which computer runs the board's work, and what that computer runs the board's runtimes as. Described in full below.

Whatever you type into a card, a memory file, or a release note is stored as you wrote it. Treat a shared board the way you would treat any shared document: if a card body would be sensitive in a wiki, it is sensitive here too.

### What an event holds

Turning notifications on for a board sends Cloud one **event** per task that needs a person: a task that reaches `ready`, or one that raises a question only you can answer. An event holds that task's **number, title, release, and current revision**, the decision it asks for, and — when it is a question — **each question with its options and the board's recommendation**.

It also holds enough of the card to review it away from your machine: the card's **opening paragraph** and its **review notes** — the sections a card writes under *Worth noting* — shortened before they are sent. Approving a build off a title alone is not a review, and the machine that raised the event may be switched off when you read it. Everything else stays behind: not the plan, not the todo list, not the rest of the body, and never the folder the board is in. Your board is registered under an identifier that means nothing outside Cloud, and where that board sits on your machine is held on that machine alone.

Once you act on an event, Cloud also holds **the decision you made and the answers you gave**, and **how the run it started ended**.

An event is kept while it is unresolved, and **deleted 30 days after it reaches a final outcome** — completed, failed, cancelled, interrupted, or retired because the task stopped needing you. Deleting it takes its answers, its outcome, and any message it was delivered as with it.

### What a board's machine record holds

A board runs its work on one computer, so that computer registers itself with Cloud — otherwise an approval you press in Slack has nowhere to run. The record holds three things and no more:

- **A machine identifier** the computer generated for itself. It is tied to no account and to nothing about your hardware, and it means nothing outside Cloud.
- **The name that computer calls itself** — its hostname, so you can tell one of your machines from another. Never an address.
- **What it runs the board's runtimes as** — for each runtime your board names, the **name of the coding agent** that computer runs it with, and the **name of the model** you set for it where you set one. Names only: never a key, never the arguments or the path a command runs under, and never the folder your board is in.

The record is kept for as long as the board is on Cloud, and goes when the board or the workspace goes. Moving the board to another machine retires the old computer's record rather than deleting it, so a job that machine started and never finished can still be accounted for.

### What never reaches Cloud

Cloud coordinates a board. It does not run your project, and the following never leave your machine:

- **Your source code**, your repository, your working tree, your branches, your commits, and your merges.
- **Your credentials** — Git remotes, SSH keys, tokens, and anything in your environment.
- **Your model provider keys and accounts.** Your coding agent runs locally, under your own account with your own provider. The board's machine record names which agent and model a computer runs — the names alone, never anything you sign in with.
- **Agent transcripts and prompts.** A run happens on your machine; Cloud sees the card it produced, not the conversation that produced it.

**No AI model is ever run over your board content.** We do not send cards, memory files, or any other workspace content to an AI provider, and we do not use your board content to train any model, ours or anyone else's.

### What signing in stores about you

A Cloud member signs in with a GitHub account, through Supabase Auth's GitHub provider. Our GitHub OAuth app requests **`user:email` and nothing else**, so a sign-in reads your profile and the email address GitHub has verified for you, and cannot reach a private repository even if everything else went wrong. We ask for the address because it is what an invitation code is sent to, so nothing about asking for one has to be typed.

Signing in creates one account record, held by Supabase Auth on our behalf. It holds:

- **Your GitHub login and public profile** as GitHub returns it at sign-in — your username, your numeric GitHub id, your display name, and your avatar URL.
- **Any email address Supabase Auth records with that account.** GitHub's sign-in response can include the address on your GitHub account, and Supabase Auth stores it with the account record when it does.
- **Sign-in timestamps** and the identity provider behind the account, which is `github` for every member.

We do not ask you to type that address, we have no mailing list, and we send nothing to it but the invitation loop below. You can ask us to remove it, or to delete the account record entirely, at [support@ai4kanban.dev](mailto:support@ai4kanban.dev).

Beyond that record, a workspace stores your member row — the account it points at, your role, and when you joined — and your name against the changes you made, which is the point of a shared board.

### Asking for an invite, and the code that answers

Cloud is invite-only, so there are two records behind getting in, and both hold your email address:

- **An invite request** — written when you press **Request an invite** in the app. It holds your GitHub handle, the email address GitHub has verified for your account, and when you asked. You type none of it: it is read from what the identity provider attested at sign-in.
- **An invitation** — written when we approve a request, or when we invite somebody who never asked. It holds the code, the address it was sent to, the request it answers, and — once redeemed — which account redeemed it and when. That is what ties an admitted account back to the invitation that let it in.

We email you once from each: a code, sent to that address from `invites@send.ai4kanban.dev`, with `support@ai4kanban.dev` to reply to. A request also sends **us** a notice at `support@ai4kanban.dev` carrying your handle and address, so we can answer you by replying. **These are the only emails the preview sends.** There is no mailing list, no announcement, and no marketing.

The mail goes through **Resend**, which is therefore a subprocessor and appears in the table below. Resend keeps 30 days of sending logs under its own policy.

### Where it runs, and who can read it

- **The workspace database** is a Supabase Postgres project in **`eu-central-1` (Frankfurt, Germany)**, inside the EU. It holds Cloud's boards and nothing else.
- **The API** runs on Cloudflare Workers at `api.ai4kanban.dev`.
- **Every workspace is private.** Signing in is not membership: every endpoint that reads a workspace or writes to it answers that workspace's members and nobody else. There is no public workspace, no shared link, and no read-only web view.
- **The database is not a client API.** Only the service can reach it: its tables are closed to every role, the project serves only the small set of functions the service calls, and its keys never leave the service.
- **We can technically reach the database**, as the operator of any hosted service can. We access workspace content only when it is necessary to run the Service, to investigate a fault, or where the law requires it — not to read your board.

### Slack, and anywhere else you choose to send board content

You can connect a Slack destination so that a question or a review request is posted into a channel — or a direct message — you already read, and decided from there. When you do that, the content of that message — the card's title, its opening paragraph, its review notes, the question being asked, and a link back into the app — leaves Cloud and goes to Slack **on your own instruction**. Anyone who can see that conversation can read it.

We do not present Slack as infrastructure AI4Kanban runs on: you install our Slack app into your own workspace, choose the one conversation it posts to, and can disconnect it at any time, which removes the connection and the token it was posting with. Once a message is in Slack, it is governed by your Slack workspace's own settings and retention policy, not by ours. The same is true of anything else you choose to point a workspace at.

What we hold for a connection is the workspace, the conversation you picked, the Slack account you connected as — which is what makes a decision you press there yours — and the token Slack issued us for posting. We never read a Slack message, including our own, and we ask for no scope that would let us.

### Deleting a workspace

An owner can delete a workspace from the app. Deletion **takes effect at once**: the one confirmed operation removes the workspace and everything in it — cards, memory, releases, history, membership, and the audit trail — with no grace window and no recovery period behind it.

Because **the preview keeps no backups of its own**, no copy of a deleted workspace survives anywhere else for us to restore, hand over, or be compelled to produce. An export a team took beforehand is the only copy that outlives the workspace, and it lives on their machines, not ours.

## Data retention

We keep personal information only as long as we need it for the purposes described in this Policy:

- **Site visits** — nothing is retained by us. Cloudflare's own delivery logs are kept under Cloudflare's policy and are not ours to query.
- **App use** — nothing is retained, because nothing is collected.
- **Workspace content** — kept for as long as the workspace exists. Deleting the workspace removes it at once, and no backup of it survives (see above). A workspace whose preview access ends is closed under the [Terms](/terms).
- **Your account record** — kept while your account can sign in. Ask us at [support@ai4kanban.dev](mailto:support@ai4kanban.dev) and we will delete it.
- **Your invite request and your invitation** — kept for as long as the account is admitted, because the invitation is what ties an admission back to the code that made it. Removing an account takes its request and its invitation with it. Resend's own sending logs are kept for 30 days under Resend's policy.
- **Events and actions** — kept while the event is unresolved, then deleted 30 days after it reaches a final outcome, together with the decision recorded against it, its outcome, and any message it was delivered as.
- **A board's machine record** — the machine identifier, the hostname, and the runtimes described above, kept while the board is on Cloud and deleted with the board or the workspace. A computer the board has moved away from keeps a retired record until then.
- **The audit trail** — an attributed record of who changed what lives inside the workspace and goes with it when the workspace is deleted.
- **Email correspondence** — kept for as long as needed to handle the conversation and any follow-up, then deleted.

We may keep limited information longer where we need it to comply with a legal obligation, resolve a dispute, or enforce our agreements.

## Security

We use reasonable administrative and technical safeguards to protect personal information:

- **Encryption** — workspace data is encrypted at rest by our database provider, and every connection to the Service uses TLS 1.2 or later.
- **A closed database** — the database denies every caller but the service, exposes only the functions the service calls, and its keys are held in the service's secret store and never in our source code.
- **Asymmetric sign-in tokens** — the service verifies a session against the project's published signing keys, so there is no shared signing secret for anyone to hold or leak.
- **A minimal-scope OAuth app** — the GitHub app behind sign-in asks for `user:email` and nothing more, so a compromised session still cannot read a private repository.
- **Least privilege** — access to production systems is limited to the people who need it.

No system is perfectly secure. If we experience a breach that materially affects you, we will notify you in line with applicable law.

Because this is a free preview running on free infrastructure tiers with **no backups**, do not treat a Cloud workspace as your only copy of anything you cannot lose. Export it.

## International data transfers

A Cloud workspace's board is stored in **Frankfurt, Germany**, inside the EU. An EU team's board therefore stays in the EU at rest.

Two transfers can still happen, and we would rather name them than leave them implied:

- **The API runs on Cloudflare's global network.** A request is handled close to where it was made, so a member working outside the EEA has their request processed in that region in transit before it reaches the database in Frankfurt.
- **Our subprocessors are US companies.** Cloudflare, Supabase, Resend, and GitHub may access data from the United States for support and operations, under their own commitments. Resend sends from the United States, so an invitation to an EU address is processed there in transit.

Where personal information is transferred out of the UK, the EEA, or Switzerland, we rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses, the UK's International Data Transfer Agreement or Addendum, the EU–US and UK–US Data Privacy Framework where a recipient is certified, or another safeguard recognized under applicable law. Copies of these safeguards are available on request.
