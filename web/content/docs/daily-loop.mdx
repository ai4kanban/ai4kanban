Everything you *say* below goes to a coding agent, which needs the skill in the repo —
`akb skill install`, or **Configuration → General** in the board app. Every ask is also a button
in the app and a command you can type (`akb --help`).

## Two ways the work gets done

**In the conversation you're in.** Ask your coding agent for a board action — "refine #4",
"#4 is done" — and it does the job right there. It already has your code in context, nothing
extra is billed, and "not that one" is your next message when it takes a wrong turn.

**Sent off as a run.** Press a button on the board, or type `akb card refine 4`, and a second
agent starts on its own. It keeps working when you close the terminal, shows in the runs panel
with its own log, and can be stopped. Nothing you say reaches it while it goes.

Send work off when you want it happening while you do something else, or for long jobs —
proposing across the repo, planning a release. Otherwise just ask.

## Pick what to work on

Say **"What's next?"** for up to three cards that are ready and unblocked, ranked by priority,
ROI and release, each with one line on why. Nothing is written. When nothing qualifies it says
what's in the way.

Planning judges every card against `memory/project.md` — what the project is and who it is for.
The board keeps it up to date; to steer direction, edit the Planner's `decisions.md` in
**Memory**, or [give an agent a rule](/docs/agents).

To think out loud instead — what a card means, whether two cards are the same — open a
[chat](/docs/chat).

## Add a task

Say **"add a task: `<idea>`"**. The skill weighs the idea's value, feasibility and overlap with
existing cards, then writes one card in plain language with checkable todos.

## Push a card forward

Say **"refine #4"**. Planning settles what the code and sources can answer, calls in the
[specialists](/docs/agents) the card needs, and leaves only your calls as open questions.

- **Nothing for you?** The card is ready to build.
- **Something for you?** Answer the questions on the card page and the board applies your
  answers.
- **Planning never builds.** You start the build.

Say **"resolve #4"** to work through a card's open questions: the skill decides what the
evidence settles and asks you the rest.

You rarely have to ask: a card you add, revise or resolve comes back refined on its own.

## Build a card

Press **Build** on the card page, or say **"build #4"**. An agent builds it, the board lands the
work as one commit on the branch you are on, and archives the card. What you approve is the
**card**, not the diff.

- **Open questions**: the card is built, then waits at landing until you answer — or press
  **Skip** on a question you don't want to answer.
- **A pause**: the card's title band says what it is waiting on and what answers it.
- **Manual commit**: untick **Build this on a branch of its own** and the agent builds in your
  folder; the card is archived once you commit it.
- **Workflows that make files** — Product video, Slide deck, Carousel post, Blog post, and your
  own with **Use a Git worktree** off — work in your project and commit nothing. Accept the
  result with **Archive**.
- **No AI review**: your own checks gate the landing. **Suggest follow-up work** catches what a
  build missed.
- **Your own commits on that branch are refused while it lands.** Commit on another branch, or
  use `git commit --no-verify`.

In a terminal it is `akb card implement 4`.

## Queue a card that is waiting on another

A card blocked by another can still be built or refined — the board only warns. By default it
is refined once the last card in its way leaves the board; the card reads **Scheduled**, with
**cancel**. `akb raw schedule 4 --action implement` makes it build instead.

## Review the board

Say **"review the board"** (or "review #4"). The skill checks cards for plain language, split
todos, duplication, and whether any are done or no longer worth it.

## Finish a task

A card built by **Build** finishes itself once it has landed.

For work done another way, say **"#4 is done"**. The skill updates the docs the change touched,
adds a line to `readme.md`, and archives the card. Archived cards stay readable in
`docs/kanban/.archive/` for a while, then are deleted; on a Local board git keeps their history.

### Test cases

Once cards finish, the **QA manager**, Coding's scheduled agent, writes and updates test cases:
what your users actually do with the product, step by step, each step proved with a screenshot,
GIF or log.

- **Where they are**: `docs/qa/<module>/<case>/case.md`, indexed in `docs/qa/README.md`. Their
  screenshots, GIFs and logs stay on your computer, in `.akb/qa/`, and never enter git.
- **Read them on the board**: **Test cases**, at the foot of the rail (under **More** on a
  phone). **Send to Triage** turns a feedback note into a triage item.
- **What a run does**: it updates the cases the latest finished cards affect, then covers one
  more module.
- **A case that fails** goes to [Triage](/docs/triage), naming the case and step.
- **How often**: set it on QA manager's page under **Scheduled** in **Configuration → Workflows
  → Coding**, or **Disable** it. See [Run an agent on a schedule](/docs/agents#run-an-agent-on-a-schedule).

## Reject an idea

Say **"reject #4"**. The card moves to the archive marked **Rejected**, with your reason kept on
it word for word. A lasting reason also goes into `rejected.md`, so the idea isn't proposed
again.

To drop it without a reason or memory, press **Remove** on the card page and leave the reason
empty — the button reads **Discard**.

## Keep it lean

Ask the skill to **prune** the memory: it compresses `readme.md`, `decisions.md`,
`rejected.md` and `redesign.md` to short summaries grouped by topic.
