Every flow the board starts is run by a named **agent**, and each one has a page: its rule, what
it remembers, its settings, and — for one you added — its own instructions. Where you reach that
page says who assigns the agent. **Configuration → Workflows** picks a workflow and shows its whole
flow top to bottom: Plan, then Execute, each with its lead and the hooks that run after it, and
under them the agents it runs on a schedule. **Configuration → Board**
lists the agents the board runs itself, which no workflow assigns and every workflow gets.

Product video, Slide deck, Carousel post and Blog post, and copies of them, need a Pro plan: sign in under
**Configuration → Cloud**, and upgrade under **Configuration → Billing**.
Coding and the workflows you make yourself stay free.

There are two kinds.

**Roles** are the agents the board's own flows are run by. You do not add or remove one, and none
can be switched off:

- **Software planner** (`software-planner`, `planner` before) — plans and refines the Coding
  workflow's cards. It runs create, refine, resolve, revise, the release
  plan, the changelog, archive and reject.
- **Builder** — builds cards and lands them: implement, conflict, run.
- **Scriptwriter** — leads the Product video workflow: a product intro, a screen demo, or both.
  It writes the script — audience, core claim, the story in viewing order, the exact narration
  and on-screen text, and the product facts with how each was checked — plus, only for a demo, what
  each demo section must prove, worded from the demo rehearser's real results and naming the
  result each one draws on. A real demo goes in only when you ask for one or it shows the point
  better than screenshots, animation or text. With Pro, narration uses a hosted
  voice in any language, spending 1 of Pro's 5,000 monthly AI credits per second: describe one, or
  pick from two or three samples.
  You review the script before the film is made; a script change, voice included, needs your
  review again. The finished film is checked and on the card with no further question, and it
  becomes the example for that kind of video — script, sources, cover, keyframes and how to remake
  every asset — so your next one starts from it.
- **Deck planner** — leads the Slide deck workflow in two rounds: first the content — audience,
  goal, facts, design and every slide's copy — for your approval, then every slide rendered from
  an editable `.pptx`, with a download on the card. A content change sends it back to the first
  round; a visual-only change rebuilds just the slides it touches.
- **Carousel planner** — leads the Carousel post workflow: one multi-page post for Xiaohongshu,
  Instagram, LinkedIn or TikTok, in two rounds like the Deck planner. First the brief, the format
  and every page's copy; then every page rendered as an image, one set per aspect ratio the
  platforms need (3:4 for Xiaohongshu, 4:5 for Instagram and LinkedIn), plus each platform's
  caption. In-page images it generates spend Pro's AI credits. It keeps one template and one best
  example per format, so your next post of that kind starts from the last one it finished.
- **Blog planner** — leads the Blog post workflow in two rounds: first the brief, the keywords you
  want to win and the outline, for your approval; then the full article on the card, with its
  images and its links to your other posts and to primary sources, and a download. For a blog in
  your project it also writes the post and its images into the blog's folder, uncommitted. It asks
  where your blog lives once and remembers.
- **Proposer** — reads a card the board has just finished, with the discussion it came from, and
  proposes the work that should follow it — including anything the card promised that nothing
  shipped. It learns from follow-ups you point out it missed. Runs after every archive.
- **Memory pruner** — squeezes the memory back down to what helps planning. Every 7 days by
  default; **Run now** on its page starts one.
- **Review chat memory** — reads back over the conversations that said something new and writes
  down what they settled. Once a day.
- **Describe the project** — keeps `docs/kanban/memory/project.md` up to date: what the project is,
  who it is for, the problems it solves and its features, from the users' point of view. Checks
  once a day and rewrites the file only when new commits have landed. Planning reads it for
  direction.

**Configuration → Board** groups them by what starts each one: **You start**, **On a schedule** and
**On an event**. A scheduled agent's page sets its cadence; the first scheduled pass lands one
cadence after the board first sees it. The Proposer and the scheduled agents each spend runs you
never asked for.

**Specialists** fill one part of a card's spec and fill only that part. Each one runs on its own,
while a card is being planned — never while it is being built — writes one named section of that
card, and changes nothing else. The board ships nine, and `akb spec` lists them:

- **`copywriting`** — writes the promotional copy a card changes: website pages, the README,
  release notes, and store or Product Hunt listings. User documentation, text inside the product
  and social posts are not its job. It writes the final copy and leaves one question asking you
  to confirm it.
- **`ui-designer`** — draws the screen a card needs as one design, one drawing per page or state
  it changes and each named for what it shows, with nothing written under the drawings. Every
  **mockup** is a rendered screen — a copy of the product's own screen, true to the real UI. See
  "Assets on a card" in `kanban-ui/README.md`. On a card that has copy, it waits until you have
  confirmed the copy and then uses it word for word.
- **`tech-stack-advisor`** — picks the library, tool, or service a card leans on. Its answer is
  one table — a row per candidate, with what it is, its pros and its cons — and one line naming
  the pick. Keeping what the project already uses and writing it yourself are rows too, so a card
  doesn't come back with something new to install by default.
- **`prompt-writer`** — writes the instruction text a card changes: a skill, an agent prompt, or
  anything else an AI model follows. Its answer is one diff per file, so you edit the wording
  directly.
- **`email-planner`** (Email planner) — writes every email a card adds or changes, in the
  language the product sends it in: the subject, the preview text and the body, with the
  HTML version previewed on desktop and mobile beside the plain text. The builder ships it
  as written. Cards that change only how mail is delivered skip it.
- **`demo-rehearser`** (Demo rehearser) — on a Product video card with a demo only, before you
  review the script: rehearses each demo section for real in an isolated environment, records the
  steps in `demo.md`, and shows each section's result with its key screenshots (3–6 in total) on
  the card for you to review.
- **`hyperframes-editor`** (Video editor) — on a Product video card only, after you approve the
  script: makes the film while the card is still being planned. It picks its own method
  (HyperFrames by default), captures the real product, generates the narration, renders on your
  machine, then watches and listens to check facts, readability, pacing, sound and anything
  private on screen. The playable video lands on the card with the command that renders it again.
- **`cover-designer`** (Cover designer) — on a Product video card only, right after the editor: makes
  one cover in the video's shape from the approved script, laid out from real screenshots and
  frames or, with Pro, generated by an image model from those screenshots at 320 AI credits each,
  and shows it on the card below the film. It asks you which way to make it alongside the script
  review, and redoes the cover when the title, core claim or brief changes.
- **`blog-illustrator`** (Blog illustrator) — on a Blog post card only, after you approve the
  outline: makes the cover and every image the outline names, new for that post — a screenshot of
  the product, a drawn diagram, or, with Pro, a generated image — each with its alt text.

One more runs on a schedule instead of during planning:

- **`qa-manager`** (QA manager) — once a day, updates the cases that the cards finished since its
  last run affect in the project's [QA manual](/docs/daily-loop#the-qa-manual) under `docs/qa/`,
  each step with its proof, then writes the first cases for one module not covered yet.

A specialist's run is a run like any other, with three things that make it different: it starts
clean, given the card and a short note but never the conversation that asked for it; it writes one
named section — ``## By `ui-designer` agent`` — and nothing else, rewritten rather than doubled on a
second run; and nothing waits for it, so a planning flow carries on and the section arrives when it
is done. When the answer is a pick only you can make, it leaves an open question instead of choosing.

You rarely ask for one yourself — the flow working the card asks when the card needs it, after a
card is added, after a refine or revise that moved the screen or the library, and never from a
release plan (each card's own refine asks instead). A card that needs neither a
screen nor a library goes through all of those without a spec run, and nothing tells you it did.

A Product video, Slide deck, Carousel post or Blog post has no build and asks you only about
the content: once the finished work is on the card, the card shows **Archive**, and archiving it
is how you accept it. **Revise** changes it instead — a content change needs your approval again, a
visual-only change keeps the content and renders again — and Archive returns when the revision is
done.

When you do want one: **"put the ui-designer agent on #4"**, or `akb spec ui-designer 4`. Whether
it joins a card's planning at all is its workflow's plan stage, under **Configuration → Workflows**:
add it there and it is offered, remove it and nothing asks for it. Setting what it produces is on
its page, beside that same list. A role is always on except the ones above, because a board without
nobody plans anything.

## Let the Proposer look back

Nothing on the board looks at finished work. A follow-up surfaces only when a build or a plan
notices one mid-task, and **Triage** reads outside sources — never your own history.

**Proposer** closes that gap. Every finished card that reaches the archive (a rejected one
starts none) starts one reflection over
that card alone: it reads what shipped, judges what work follows from it against `project.md` and the
planning memory, and puts each survivor in **Triage**.

- **Archiving is the only trigger.** A card completing is what starts a reflection. Nothing sweeps
  the cards you finished last week, and there is no command that asks for one.
- **One run per completion.** That is the whole of what it costs.
- **It proposes into Triage, never onto the board.** A proposal is triaged like anything else
  that arrives there, so a weak one costs you a dismissal rather than a card in your backlog.
  With Pro, Triage [sorts new items by itself](/docs/triage#let-it-sort-by-itself).
- **It grounds every proposal.** Anything already planned, already in Triage, or in a
  `rejected.md` is skipped. Finding nothing worth proposing is a normal result, and the common one.

Each item it writes carries a short rationale and the card that prompted it, so you can read what
the proposal came from before deciding.

## Let the memory review remember for you

A chat used to judge each turn as it landed: was this a preference worth remembering? One turn
cannot see where a conversation is going, so a "what if" you threw out and took back three turns
later could land in `decisions.md` as a decision — while the real conclusion of a long discussion
was never written down at all.

**Review chat memory** is what writes it down instead. Chats no longer save memory. Once a card
is archived, it reads that card's chat right through, once, and writes what the whole exchange
earned.

- **After the card is done.** Memory only feeds future planning; the card you are talking about
  was already changed in the conversation.
- **Each conversation once.** It is read whole when its card is archived and never again, so say
  what should last before then.
- **Cleared chats count too.** A card chat you cleared, or restarted on another agent, is kept
  on your machine and read with the rest once the card is archived, then deleted — or after 30
  days unread.
- **Discussions are not read.** What a discussion settled is in the cards it became; repeat a
  preference that should last in a card's chat.
- **"Don't record this" holds.** Say it in the conversation and nothing is written.
- **It credits the right agent.** A correction to one agent's work goes in that agent's memory;
  the rest goes in the planning memory, under the `## <module>` topic the card's `modules:` names.
- **Nothing archived, nothing run.** It starts at most one round a day, ten cards to a run, and
  keeps going until none are waiting.
- **What it skips.** A rejected card's chat, and a discussion that never became a card.

**Review now** on its page asks for one at any time, and `akb review-memory` is the same entry
from a terminal. What each review wrote is in its run log.

## Give an agent a rule

A **rule** is one paragraph, in your own words, added to the **end** of every run that agent does,
after everything the board writes into it. Write it in the box on the agent's page; it saves when
you leave the box.

- **It is plain words, not a command the board runs.** What it actually causes is up to the agent
  reading it.
- **It belongs to the agent, not to one flow.** One rule on the Builder reaches implement, conflict
  and run alike, so a convention you write once is followed everywhere that agent works.
- **It lives in your board**, at `docs/kanban/rules/<agent>.md`, tracked in git — so a team shares
  it and a run started from a terminal reads the same words. Clearing the box deletes the file.
- **A delivery freezes the rules it starts with**, so editing one changes the next delivery and
  never one in flight.
- **Every session that agent starts reads it**, so a long rule makes every card slower.

From a terminal: `akb raw rule <agent> --file <path>`, or `--text ""` to clear it.

## Give an agent memory

Some agents **remember** — the taste they were corrected on, and the choices you made. The
agent's page lists the files it has, read-only:

- **Planning** keeps three files in `docs/kanban/memory/agents/planner/`: `decisions.md`,
  `rejected.md` and `redesign.md`. Whoever leads a plan stage writes them;
  the Proposer reads them and writes none. No other role owns a file.
- An **agent** owns `docs/kanban/memory/agents/<name>/`, and its own `AGENT.md` says which
  files it keeps there and what each holds.
  `ui-designer` keeps `redesign.md` and `decisions.md`, `copywriting` keeps `writing.md`, and
  `tech-stack-advisor` keeps none. A specialist is handed every file in its folder on each run.
- **The deck planner remembers your feedback** in `feedback.md`, so the next deck starts from it.
- **The video agents remember your feedback.** Tell the scriptwriter or the video editor what to
  do differently and the next video starts from it; one video's change is not a general rule.
  Nothing you say is written back into a recipe. Long feedback is split
  into `feedback/<topic>.md`; the Memory panel lists those files under **Feedback**, and the
  index in `feedback.md` links to them.
- **The board's own record is nobody's memory.** `docs/kanban/memory/readme.md` — what shipped —
  and `project.md` are facts rather than taste, so they sit outside every agent's folder.
- **How the product looks is not memory.** Colours, dimensions and component detail are read from
  the app's own `design.md`, so memory says which doc to read rather than repeating it.
- The runs write these themselves — the flow that hears your answer records what was taken or sent
  back, and the agent curates the files on its own runs. Nothing on the pane edits one.

## Add one of your own

Creating a custom agent requires the [AI4Kanban skill](/docs/daily-loop) in your project and the
[`akb` CLI](/docs#install-the-board). Follow the linked setup instructions if either is missing.

In your project's coding agent session, describe the agent you need:

```text
Use the AI4Kanban skill. Read akb guide write-agent,
then create an agent that [describe its job].
```

That guide is the one place the file is written from — its name, its description, the files beside
it and the translations it carries — so what comes back is an agent this board accepts.
`akb spec` lists the agents this board can use and, under them, anything wrong with one it found.

The **+** beside **After planning**, **After executing** or under **Scheduled** in Configuration →
Workflows starts one from the board instead: it asks for a name, writes the folder under
`docs/kanban/agents/`, adds it there, and opens the new agent's page with its instructions box focused. A name a role or a
bundled agent already answers to is refused in place. The whole file is the box on that page,
frontmatter included, and a save the board would refuse
keeps your text and says what is wrong rather than leaving an agent it cannot read — the pane and
the file are one thing.

A new agent is a **hook**: it runs after its stage, or by itself on a schedule.

- **After planning**: the lead calls it in when its description applies to the card.
- **After executing**: it runs once after every build, in the order listed, in the folder holding
  the build. Its changes are committed with the delivery, and the work is delivered only when the
  last hook has finished. A hook that fails or is stopped stops the delivery; **Resume** on its
  run, or `akb delivery resume <id>`, carries on from that hook.
- **Scheduled** (`akb.hook: schedule`): it runs by itself, once per schedule, on no card — see
  below.

## Run an agent on a schedule

A workflow can carry agents that run by themselves: a nightly audit, a weekly clean-up. Each sits
under **Scheduled** in Configuration → Workflows, below the two stages, and belongs to that
workflow alone. Coding ships one, running every day: the
[QA manager](/docs/daily-loop#the-qa-manual).

Recurring cards have moved here. On update, each one becomes a scheduled agent of its workflow:
its steps are the agent's instructions, its cadence and last run carry over, and a card with no
cadence arrives disabled.

- **Set it on the agent's page.** The chip beside its name picks how often — every 6 hours, every
  day, every 7 days, or your own — and **Disable** is the last row of the same list. Picking a
  schedule again enables it. **Run now** starts one run whether or not it is due, and works
  while the agent is disabled.
- **It starts every time its schedule comes round**, whether or not there is anything to do. A
  quiet day still costs one short run.
- **It never runs the moment you enable it.** The first run comes one schedule later; the page
  shows the last run and when the next may start.
- **What it changes is committed like a build.** With **Automatic Git commits** on, the run works
  on its own branch and lands on your current branch; a run that changed nothing commits nothing.
  With it off, the changes stay uncommitted in your project. A workflow that makes files commits
  nothing.
- **Nobody answers it.** A scheduled agent is not a card: it has no open questions, and what it
  does with something it cannot settle is for its own instructions to say.
- **A failed run waits.** Only a run that finished moves the last-run time; a failed or stopped one
  is tried again one schedule later, or **Resume** it from **Runs**, where each run is named by its
  workflow and agent.
- **It can ask what finished since last time.** Inside its run,
  `akb raw list --archived --since last-run` lists the cards that landed since its last finished
  run began — none on the first run.

From a terminal: `akb workflow schedule <id> --on <agent> --cadence 1d`, `--off <agent>`, or
`--run <agent>`; `akb workflow list` shows each one's schedule and last run.

It can be written to **lead** a plan or execute stage instead, and one that can lead is only
offered as a workflow's lead, never as a hook. Of the ones the board ships, the Software planner,
the Builder and the Scriptwriter lead. A workflow that already has an undeclared agent leading, or
a lead among its hooks, keeps running until you change it, and Configuration → Workflows marks an
undeclared lead.

**Delete** on an agent's page removes it and everything the board kept for it — the folder, the
instructions you wrote for it, what it remembered, and its switch. Only an agent this project
added: the roles and the three the command ships are not yours to remove, and have no Delete.
